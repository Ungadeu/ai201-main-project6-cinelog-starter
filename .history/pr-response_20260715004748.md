## Comment 5 — Sort Order
**My position:** I am siding with @dev-lead and changing the default sort order to "date-added" (newest first).

**Reasoning:** To borrow a concept from managing massive storage rooms in Minecraft, when you dump your inventory after a long mining session, you usually want the newest blocks right at the top so you can grab them quickly for your next base build. In a movie tracking app, a user's most recently added films represent their "active quests"—the movies they are currently hyped to watch. Sorting alphabetically (putting *Alien* before *Zootopia*) scatters these new additions across the entire inventory, forcing the player to hunt for the exact item they just picked up.

**Engagement with reviewer's point:** While I initially leaned towards alphabetical sorting for a clean, encyclopedia-style inventory look, your point about practical user behavior is spot on. 'Date-added' optimizes for the player's immediate goals and active interests rather than just alphabetical neatness, so I have updated the database query to reflect this change.

## PR Description

**Feature Overview:**
This PR introduces the Watchlist feature, acting as a personal "Quest Log" for players. It allows users to save films they want to watch later, distinct from the collection of films they have already completed. The feature includes a superhero bouncer (deduplication logic) to ensure players cannot accidentally add the same movie to their inventory twice.

**Design Decisions:**
1. **Visibility Default (public=True):** Watchlists are public by default to encourage passive multiplayer interaction and community film discovery, though a UI toggle is recommended for a future update to ensure player privacy controls.
2. **Sort Order (Date-Added):** The inventory automatically sorts by the date added (newest first). This optimizes for active quests, ensuring the newest items a player picks up are right at the top of their chest rather than buried alphabetically.

**Manual Testing Steps:**
1. Boot up the local server by running python app.py in your terminal.
2. Trigger the add_to_watchlist endpoint with a valid user ID and film ID to ensure the item successfully equips to the database.
3. Attempt to add the exact same film again to verify that the AlreadyInWatchlistError deploys correctly.
4. Retrieve the watchlist and confirm that the most recently added film is sitting at the very top of the list.
5. Finally, run python -m pytest tests/ -v to ensure the dummy test trains pass with flying colors.
